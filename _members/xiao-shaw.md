I am a PhD student in Computer Science at McMaster University, supervised by Dr. Christian Brodbeck.

My research focuses on computational modeling of the human brain, with the goal of understanding how neural systems process information and give rise to human cognition. I am particularly interested in developing artificial neural network models and investigating the relationship between their internal representations and brain activity measured using EEG and MEG. By connecting computational models with neural signals, I aim to better understand the mechanisms underlying human information processing, particularly in speech and language comprehension.

My long-term research goal is to explore non-invasive brain–computer interfaces that use artificial intelligence to decode and interpret neural activity without requiring surgical implants. By integrating computational brain models with neural recordings, I hope to develop systems capable of identifying meaningful patterns and changes in brain function. Ultimately, I envision these technologies contributing to the early detection of neurological disorders, continuous monitoring of brain health, and the development of personalized rehabilitation and therapeutic approaches.
