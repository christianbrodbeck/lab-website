I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Computing and Software. I am currently working on an [NIH](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/MO5MEigAnEeHz2DkOCOs0g/project-details/10864540)-funded project developing a user-friendly data analysis pipeline built on neurocurrent response functions (NCRFs). I use existing magnetoencephalography (MEG) datasets and computational models to trace how the human brain turns a stream of sound into meaning.

I completed my PhD in Cognitive Robotics at the Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology Graduate University (OIST) in 2024, an MS in Applied Cognition and Neuroscience at the University of Texas at Dallas in 2014, and a BTech in Biotechnology at Anna University in 2012. My PhD work focused on brain-inspired neural networks that let robots learn language and action together. I developed a network architecture based on the Free Energy Principle, trained it with multimodal data from an arm robot, and showed how it combines familiar concepts in new ways, a hallmark of compositionality. Earlier in my career, I studied ghrelin and related questions in metabolic neuroscience, including exercise endurance, feeding, and glucose regulation.

Broadly, I am interested in how minds, biological and artificial, come to understand language. In the lab, I work with existing MEG datasets of people listening to natural, continuous speech. I convert them to the BIDS format and analyze them with [Eelbrain](https://eelbrain.readthedocs.io) and NCRFs, modeling how acoustic and linguistic features of the speech drive activity across cortical regions over time. My goal is to understand how the brain transforms external signals into abstract linguistic representations and combines them into larger structures.
